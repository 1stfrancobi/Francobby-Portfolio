UNDERGRADUATE RESEARCH · UMAT · 2025

Research overview

This project investigated how machine-learning models could estimate missing observations in GRACE/GRACE-FO satellite-gravity time series.

The work combined numerical data analysis, predictive modelling, model comparison and scientific reporting. Its central question was how reliably a model could reconstruct missing values from patterns and relationships in the available data.

The research problem

Gaps in satellite observations interrupt the continuity of a time series and can complicate analysis of changes over time. The transition between GRACE and its follow-on mission created a period for which a continuous observational record was unavailable.

My research explored a modelling approach to this problem: use available observations to learn relationships, then produce estimates for missing periods.

My approach and contribution

I applied Python-based modelling to satellite-gravity time series, compared predictive approaches and assessed the quality of their outputs. I documented the methods, comparative findings and limitations in a scientific report.

  1. Define the analytical objective. Identify the missing observations to be reconstructed and establish how model performance would be assessed.
  2. Prepare the analytical data. Organize the time-series inputs and target observations for modelling and evaluation.
  3. Develop and compare models. Apply predictive approaches and examine differences in their ability to reproduce available observations.
  4. Evaluate and interpret outputs. Use error metrics and statistical diagnostics to assess prediction quality and identify limitations.
  5. Communicate the evidence. Explain the methodology and the reasoning behind model comparisons and conclusions.

How I evaluated the analysis

Evaluation focused on how closely predictions matched reference observations, how performance varied between models, and what those differences meant for selecting a reconstruction method. Error metrics and statistical diagnostics supported this assessment.

A model's ability to reproduce observations must be distinguished from its ability to generalize to unseen data. For time-series work, the evaluation design also needs to respect the temporal structure of the observations.

Interpreting reconstructed values

Reconstructed values are estimates rather than new satellite measurements. Performance on withheld observations provides evidence about the method, but it does not directly verify predictions inside a gap where reference observations are unavailable.

This distinction matters when communicating results: a useful reconstruction should be accompanied by a clear explanation of its assumptions and uncertainty.
